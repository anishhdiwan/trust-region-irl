Abstract

Inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) is typically formulated as maximizing entropy subject to matching the distribution of expert trajectories. Classical dual-ascent IRL guarantees monotonic improvement but requires fully solving an RL problem each iteration. Adversarial methods skip this cost — but sacrifice stability and monotonic improvement by replacing the global reward with local discriminator outputs.

We bridge the gap. Our central insight is that a trust-region-optimal policy for a reward update can be globally optimal for a smaller update in the same direction — enabling explicit dual optimization from only a local policy search. The resulting algorithm, Trust Region Inverse Reinforcement Learning (TRIRL), avoids adversarial instabilities, provides monotonic improvement guarantees, and learns a globally-optimizable reward function that can be re-optimized and transferred to new environments.

TRIRL outperforms state-of-the-art imitation learning methods by 2.4× in aggregate inter-quartile mean across challenging MuJoCo and humanoid robotics tasks, while recovering reward functions robust to system dynamics shifts.

Key Insight

The MCE-IRL dual objective is improved by iterative function-space reward updates:

$$r^{(i+1)} = r^{(i)} - \epsilon\!\left(r^{(i)} - \beta\log\frac{\rho_E(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})} {\rho_{\pi^{(i)}}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})}\right)$$

Each update requires the MCE-optimal policy $\pi^{(i)}$ — which classically demands running RL to convergence. TRIRL avoids this with a key equivalence:

Theorem (informal)
A trust-region-optimal policy for updated reward $\tilde{r}^{(i+1)}$ with step $\epsilon$ is the MCE-optimal policy for a corrected reward $r^{(i+1)}$ with a smaller step $\epsilon_\mathrm{tr} = \epsilon\,/\,(1{+}\eta)$, where $\eta \geq 0$ is the trust-region Lagrangian multiplier.

In practice: run a trust-region policy update, read off $\eta$, shrink the reward step accordingly. The resulting policy–reward pair is MCE-valid, and successive applications improve the dual monotonically — no full RL solve required.

Results

We compare against GAIL, AIRL, AMP, LSIQ, NEAR, and SFM on five MuJoCo benchmarks and three humanoid locomotion tasks (Unitree G1 Walking/Running, Unitree Go2), across 20 independent seeds. G1 tasks use state-based observations from motion-capture.

Global & Transferable Rewards

Unlike adversarial methods, TRIRL learns a global reward that can be re-optimized from a fresh policy initialization and transfers to environments with modified dynamics (Ant with disabled legs, HalfCheetah with wind, HalfCheetah with Mars gravity). TRIRL outperforms AIRL and NEAR in all retraining and transfer settings.
