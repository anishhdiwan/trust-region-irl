Abstract

Inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) is typically formulated as maximizing entropy subject to matching the distribution of expert trajectories. Classical dual-ascent IRL guarantees monotonic improvement but requires fully solving an RL problem each iteration. Adversarial methods skip this cost — but sacrifice stability and monotonic improvement by replacing the global reward with local discriminator outputs.

We bridge the gap. Our central insight is that a trust-region-optimal policy for a reward update can be globally optimal for a smaller update in the same direction. This allows us to explicitly optimize the IRL dual objective using only a local policy search around the current iterate. The resulting algorithm, TRIRL, avoids adversarial instabilities, provides monotonic improvement guarantees, and learns a globally-optimizable reward function — one that can be re-optimized from scratch and transferred to new environments.

TRIRL outperforms state-of-the-art imitation learning methods by a factor of 2.4× in aggregate inter-quartile mean across challenging MuJoCo and humanoid robotics tasks.

Key Insight

The MCE-IRL dual objective is improved by iterative function-space reward updates:

$$r^{(i+1)} = r^{(i)} - \epsilon\!\left(r^{(i)} - \beta\log\frac{\rho_E(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})}{\rho_{\pi^{(i)}}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{a})}\right)$$

Each update requires the MCE-optimal policy $\pi^{(i)}$ — which, classically, demands running RL to convergence. TRIRL avoids this with a key equivalence:

Theorem (informal).  A trust-region-optimal policy for the updated reward $\tilde{r}^{(i+1)}$ with step size $\epsilon$ is the MCE-optimal policy for a corrected reward $r^{(i+1)}$ with a smaller step $\epsilon_\mathrm{tr} = \epsilon\,/\,(1 + \eta)$, where $\eta \geq 0$ is the Lagrangian multiplier of the trust-region constraint.

In practice: run a trust-region policy update, read off the Lagrange multiplier $\eta$, and shrink the reward step accordingly. The resulting policy–reward pair is MCE-valid. Repeat to monotonically improve the dual objective — no full RL solve required.

Results

We compare against GAIL, AIRL, AMP, LSIQ, NEAR, and SFM on five MuJoCo benchmarks and three challenging humanoid locomotion tasks (Unitree G1 Walking/Running, Unitree Go2 Locomotion), reporting mean performance across 20 independent seeds. TRIRL uses motion-capture demonstrations with state-based observations for the G1 tasks.

Global & Transferable Reward Functions

Unlike adversarial methods, TRIRL learns a global reward function — one that can be re-optimized from a fresh random policy initialization and transfers to environments with modified dynamics (Ant with disabled legs, HalfCheetah with wind, HalfCheetah with Mars gravity). TRIRL outperforms AIRL and NEAR in all retraining and transfer settings.
